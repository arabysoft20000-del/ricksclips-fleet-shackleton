# Script

The script drives everything: the shots, the cuts, the world and the sound follow it. Never force it into a fixed number of scenes or a fixed running time.

## Length
Never below 112 words and never above 295 words (the shortest and the longest reference script). The story decides where it lands. Full stories usually run 198 to 295 words, short explainers 112 to 165.

## The five parts, in this order
1. **Hook** (the first sentence or two, about 15 words): a bizarre action, a counterintuitive claim, or a direct "you" scenario ("If you ever..."). The strange result comes first, with no explanation yet.
2. **Escalation:** set up the character or scenario and raise the tension. Use a run of "then... then...", steps of time or size, one exact number or clock time. The hero is doubted or laughed at.
3. **Pivot:** the hidden rule, loophole, mechanism or motive is revealed, usually with "You see". This lands late, at about 70 to 85 percent of the script. An early "You see" in sentence two may set up the world.
4. **Execution:** the trick or rule plays out.
5. **Resolution:** a twist that recasts the story, the explained mechanism, a vivid physical payoff, or an ironic closing line. A question to the viewer only rarely.

## Style
- Sentences of about 12 to 16 words, with very short punches ("Not really.") between long chained sentences. Present tense. Speak to "you" where it fits.
- At least one list of three, one everyday comparison for the hard part ("Ever blown on a bowl of soup?"), and one exact number.
- Short spoken dialogue, one to three lines, is common, usually from a doubter or the antagonist.
- Connectors people actually say: "So", "But", "Then", "You see", "Well,", "Wait, what?". A rhetorical question answered at once with "Well," works. Recurring words: nobody, every, exact, whole.
- The hero is usually just "he" or a role. A short first name or nickname only now and then.
- Plain spoken English like a person telling a story. No AI-sounding words or phrases. Examples to avoid, not a complete list: delve, tapestry, testament, realm, unleash, game-changer, moreover, furthermore, in today's world, let's dive in, buckle up.

## Format
- Plain spoken text in one block. No headings, part labels, scene or camera notes, sound notes, emojis or hashtags.
- Write all numbers as words ("fifty coins", "thirty nine feet"). Captions show them as digits later.
- English only.

## Facts
For a story based on real facts, every fact and number must match SHORT/facts.md. If a number or claim is unclear, or is not true everywhere, word it so it stays true and safe: "almost all" instead of "all", "about" or "roughly" for a figure, or leave the claim out. Do not say what you checked.

## Before showing it
Count the words (112 to 295). Check the five parts, the last line, and that no AI-sounding word slipped in. The wording must be original: never copy or closely follow an existing video's script.

## Layout
Save the script in SHORT/script.md (in one code block). Reply with the script in a copyable block:
````
Script:
```text
<full script>
```
````
If the story is made up, then one line: "This topic and script are made up, not real."
End with a closing in your own natural, professional words that says: if the script is fine, please make the voiceover and send me the audio file; or tell me what to change. Do not name any voice tool.
After a change request, show the whole changed script again with the same ending.
